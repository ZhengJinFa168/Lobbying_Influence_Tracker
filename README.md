# Lobby Echo: tracing lobbying influence on the EU AI Act

**Question.** Do stakeholder comments on the AI Act (procedure `2021/0106(COD)`) show up in the legal text, and through whom?

This repository is a data pipeline, a Neo4j graph and an interactive demo. It follows public comments from the European Commission's
*Have Your Say* portal into the Parliament's committee amendments, through the plenary report and into Regulation (EU) 2024/1689,
and measures how often wording echoed in an amendment makes it into the law, who carried it, and what the lobbies behind it look like.

- **Track A, before the bill:** 98 comments on the 2020 inception impact assessment, compared with the Commission's proposal COM(2021)206.
- **Track B, on the bill:** 237 comments on the 2021 proposal, compared with 4,852 MEP committee amendments, then the EP report (June 2023), then the final act.

> An **echo** means a comment's wording reappears in an amendment above a chance threshold and an LLM agrees. It is evidence of similar
> wording, not proof that a lobby caused it. Shared outside sources (the HLEG guidelines, the OECD definition of AI) and coalition texts produce echoes too.

## What we found

Numbers from the current run (Track B, counted per distinct amendment). An echo is a judged pair the LLM calls *implements*,
or *partial* with a verbatim run of 12+ words (see [Evidence tiers](#the-pipeline) and the null check below):

| Echoing | Amendments | Survived into the EP report | Reached the final law |
|---|---|---|---|
| NGOs | 83 | 22.9% | 6.0% |
| Companies | 75 | 30.7% | 8.0% |
| Business associations | 54 | 22.2% | 7.4% |
| All echoed amendments | 175 | 24.0% (95% CI 18.3-30.8%) | 5.1% (95% CI 2.7-9.5%) |
| **All 4,852 amendments (base rate)** | 4,852 | 20.5% | 3.6% |

*Sensitivity, previous echo rule* (verbatim, or *implements* / *partial*; used until 2026-10-23; `data/results/02b_reach_funnel_legacy_rule.csv`
and `echoed_amendments_all.csv`):
NGOs 211 amendments, 19.0% / 4.7%; companies 181, 21.5% / 3.3%; business associations 169, 16.0% / 2.4%; all echoed 430, 18.4% / 3.3%.

- **Why the rule changed:** S12 shows the judge 200 pairs of comments and amendments to an *unrelated* act that still pass the match threshold.
  It calls 0% of them *implements* but 36.5% *partial*, against 15.8% and 51% for the 2,000 judged real pairs (the strongest matches),
  so *partial* on its own is mostly the judge's background rate. The previous rule counted it and so included a large share of noise.
  A verbatim run the judge calls *contradicts* or *unrelated* is not an echo either.
- Echoed amendments survive and reach the law **somewhat more often than the base rate** (24.0% vs 20.5%; 5.1% vs 3.6%), but over all
  echoed amendments the 95% intervals include the base rate, so this is **not a clear effect**. One subgroup stands out: amendments echoing
  companies survived 30.7% of the time (95% CI 21.4-41.8%, above the base rate; one of three subgroups, not corrected for multiple
  comparisons). With the previous rule echoed amendments reached the law at about the base rate (3.3%). What the amendment changes
  (a new provision, the definitions in Art 3) and who tabled it matter more.
- **Money does not track influence overall:** across all organisations, correlations between the influence score and spend, staff, passes
  or meetings are about 0.1, and all five 95% intervals include 0. Within companies, spend does correlate (rho = 0.31, 95% CI 0.08-0.53;
  previous rule 0.33, 3 October run in `data/archive_2026-10-03/results/`).
- **Access:** 11.0% of echoed (organisation, amendment) pairs have a recorded meeting with a tabling MEP, against 7.8% for random AI Act MEPs
  (95% range 5.5-10.3%). That is just above the random range, but the meeting data has a gap and the random baseline is not seeded yet,
  so treat it as weak evidence (previous rule: 8.5% vs 7.3%, 3 October run).
- **Before the bill:** 39% of 2020 positions were adopted by the proposal, against 34% for random choices (the alignment analysis uses coded positions, not echo tiers).
- **Coalitions** appear as near-identical paragraphs, for example the digital-rights NGOs (EDRi, Digitalcourage, Access Now, Bits of Freedom).

These figures are regenerated by the pipeline; see `data/results/` after a run. Caveats are listed [below](#caveats).

## Architecture in one picture

```
 sources ──► Python loaders ──► Neo4j backbone graph ──► Python similarity / LLM ──► ECHOED_IN edges ──► Neo4j
                                        │                                                              │
                                        └──────────── Cypher + GDS analyses ◄──────────────────────────┘
                                                              │
                                              exports (CSV/JSON) ──► dashboard.html · demo.html · Neo4j Browser
```

**Neo4j is the single source of truth.** Python loads every source as nodes and relationships, does the numeric work outside the database
(embeddings, n-gram overlap, LLM judging) and writes results back as relationships. Analysis runs as Cypher and Graph Data Science.
Diagrams of the system, the stage flow, the echo funnel, the graph schema and the live demo are in
**[docs/ARCHITECTURE.md](docs/ARCHITECTURE.md)** (Mermaid, rendered on GitHub).

## The pipeline

Each stage is `pipeline/sNN_*.py`. `python -m pipeline.run` runs them in order.

| Stage | What it does | Writes |
|---|---|---|
| **S00** `s00_check` | Sanity check: sources, counts, Neo4j and plugins, schema | report |
| **S01** `s01_feedback` | Have Your Say comments: strip boilerplate, cut into ~80-150-word chunks, parse cited articles | `Organisation`, `Comment`, `Chunk`, `CITES` |
| **S02** `s02_legal_texts` | EUR-Lex HTML into legal units (recitals, articles, paragraphs, points, annexes), ids matching ParlTrack | `Provision` (proposal), `LegalUnit` (final act) |
| **S03** `s03_amendments` | ParlTrack committee amendments; keeps only the words each one inserts | `Amendment`, `MEP`, `Group`, `Committee`, `AMENDS`, `TABLED` |
| **S04** `s04_survival` | Follows each amendment into the EP report and the final act, with null-calibrated thresholds | `AdoptedAmendment`, `SURVIVED_AS`, `BECAME`, `survived`, `reached_law` |
| **S05** `s05_features` | Proposal 6-gram stoplist, TF-IDF, `multilingual-e5-base` embeddings (cached) | `data/features/`, `Chunk.embedding` |
| **S06** `s06_orgs` | Joins organisations to LobbyFacts and the Integrity Watch register (register number first, then strict name rules) | org properties |
| **S06b** `s06b_meetings` | MEP meetings from Integrity Watch matched to consultation organisations | `MET_WITH` |
| **S07** `s07_match` | Candidate (comment, target) pairs; embedding, TF-IDF and n-gram scores; near-duplicate comments | candidates, `SIMILAR_TO` |
| **S08** `s08_calibrate` | Same comments scored against the Digital Services Act amendments; threshold = 99th percentile | `ECHOED_IN`, `carrier_met` |
| **S09** `s09_judge_and_stance` | LLM judge rates each echo and assigns tiers T1/T2/T3; Track A stance coding and the proposal's own choices | verdicts, `TOOK_POSITION`, `Issue` |
| **S10** `s10_analyse` | 13 named Cypher queries (`neo4j/queries/`), GDS (PageRank, betweenness, Louvain), statistics | `data/results/*.csv` |
| **S10b** `s10b_reach_model` | Logistic models for P(survive) and P(reach the law), with explanations | `Amendment.p_*`, `reach_model.csv` |
| **S11** `s11_export_dashboard` | `graph.json` and the analysis dashboard | `dashboard.html` |
| **S11b** `s11b_demo` | The multi-page demo (lobbies, articles, stories, politicians, pre-bill vs bill) | `demo.html` |
| **S12** `s12_eval` | Cross-model agreement, null false-positive rate, verbatim sanity, stance stability | `eval.csv` |

**Evidence tiers** on an echo: **T1** verbatim (12+ identical words after the stoplist) and the judge says *implements*; **T2** *implements*, or verbatim and *partial*;
**T3** above the threshold only (including *partial* without a verbatim run, which the null check shows is mostly noise). Until 2026-10-23
T2 was "verbatim, or *implements* / *partial*"; query `02b` reports the funnel under that rule. Access (did the organisation meet an MEP who tabled the amendment?) is stored as `carrier_met` and reported *next to* the tier, never folded into it.

## Data sources

All inputs are listed in `data_sources/sources.yaml` with a status (`available` or `placeholder`).

| Source | Used for |
|---|---|
| Have Your Say (publications 13340 and 14488), scraped by `WebScraping/` | comments |
| EUR-Lex via the Publications Office CELLAR API | proposal, final act (as adopted and 2026 consolidation) |
| [ParlTrack](https://parltrack.org) dumps in `amendments/` | committee and plenary amendments, MEPs, groups |
| LobbyFacts (2026-10-03 snapshot) | spend, FTE, EP passes, Commission meetings |
| Integrity Watch EU | register data and MEP-lobby meetings (2020 and Jun-Oct 2024 only) |

## Quick start

**Needs:** Docker, Python 3.12 (we use [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/)), about 16 GB RAM, and an [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai) key for the LLM steps.

```bash
# 1. environment
uv venv --python 3.12 .venv
uv pip install --python .venv/bin/python -r requirements.txt

# 2. secrets (never committed): create .env
#    NEO4J_URI=bolt://localhost:7687  NEO4J_USER=neo4j  NEO4J_PASSWORD=<choose one>
#    OPENROUTER_API_KEY=<your key>
#    LLM_MODEL=deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash  LLM_MODEL_SECOND=google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite  LLM_MODEL_HARD=deepseek/deepseek-v4-pro

# 3. Neo4j with APOC and GDS
docker compose -f neo4j/docker-compose.yml --env-file .env up -d

# 4. run everything (or one track / one stage)
.venv/bin/python -m pipeline.run
.venv/bin/python -m pipeline.run --track B
.venv/bin/python -m pipeline.run --from s07
.venv/bin/python -m pipeline.run --only s11b

# 5. another law (see "Other laws" below), e.g. the Digital Services Act
.venv/bin/python -m pipeline.run --procedure dsa
```

The ParlTrack dumps (`amendments/*.zst`, over 100 MB) are not in git; download them from ParlTrack into `amendments/`. Embeddings need the
`intfloat/multilingual-e5-base` model, downloaded on first use.

**Time and cost.** Everything except the LLM step takes minutes (embedding the comments is about 4 minutes). The LLM judge over about 2,350 pairs plus stance coding
took about 20 minutes and cost roughly $0.25 on the cheap models; answers are cached by prompt hash, so re-runs are free. Tokens and cost are logged to `data/llm_costs.csv`.

## The demo

A prebuilt copy of the demo is committed at **`demo/demo.html`**: download it and open it in any browser, no setup needed. To rebuild it yourself, run the pipeline (S10/S10b/S11b) and open the generated files in `data/results/` (those are not in git):

```bash
open demo/demo.html                  # prebuilt demo (or data/results/demo.html after a run)
open data/results/dashboard.html    # the analysis dashboard
```

Press `/` (or ⌘K) in the demo to search lobbies, MEPs, articles and stories.

- **Overview**: the act (each build marks its own act as processed; see [Other laws](#other-laws)), headline numbers, the featured story.
- **Lobbies**: ranking by influence score; each profile shows spend, meetings, interests, their 2020 positions, where their wording went and the MEPs who carried it.
- **Articles**: click an article for a graph of the top K most similar comments, with a slider for K.
- **Stories**: one comment followed through the amendment and the EP report into the final law, with the probability of each outcome and why.
- **Politicians**: party, committees, declared outside activities, track record, meetings.
- **Pre-bill vs bill**: whether the proposal took the 2020 positions, whether echoed amendments go further, the reach model, access and money.

Two buttons call a small local proxy that keeps the API key out of the page ("Judge this pair live" and "Ask the graph"):

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m pipeline.serve        # http://localhost:8765
```

Without the proxy the pages fall back to cached verdicts and prepared queries. For Neo4j Browser, `neo4j/demo/demo_queries.cypher` has five queries to paste one at a time.

## Other laws

The pipeline started as an AI Act project. It now runs ordinary legislative procedures (COD) described by a config file. The AI Act is the
default; generalising the code was checked not to change its output (AI Act re-run on 2026-10-23), while the later echo-rule and parser
fixes changed it on purpose (see [What we found](#what-we-found)). The Digital Services Act (2020/0361(COD)) runs end to end.

### How it works now

| | Now |
|---|---|
| Which law | Any procedure with a config file `data_sources/procedures/<id>.yaml` (`ai_act.yaml`, `dsa.yaml`); the stages read every law-specific value (IDs, report, rapporteurs, dates, prompt wording, page text) from it; the demo's list of other acts in `pipeline/s11b_demo.py` is the exception |
| Running | `python -m pipeline.run` for the AI Act; `--procedure <id>` (or `PROCEDURE_ID=<id>`) for another law |
| Data and database | Each law has its own `data/<id>/` folder and its own Neo4j, set in `.env.<id>`; a run that would use the AI Act database for another law is refused. The LLM cache is shared (keyed by model, prompt, schema and seed) |
| Safety | `run.py` only runs a stage for another law if it sets `PROCEDURE_AWARE = True` (every stage does now; S00, the sanity check, always runs); config files, contracts, linter config, tests and CI are protected and change only with the owner's approval; `tach check` (pre-commit hook; the CI workflow is not on `main` yet) enforces module boundaries and currently fails on the boundary debt listed in `TASKS.md` |
| Scraping | `WebScraping/*.py --procedure <id>`; SSL via certifi; PDF and Word attachments read with pypdf, pdfplumber and python-docx (in `requirements.txt`) |
| Echo rule | T2 = *implements*, or *partial* with a verbatim run; the previous rule (verbatim, or *implements* / *partial*) is kept as a sensitivity output (query `02b`, `echoed_amendments_all.csv`) |
| Evaluation | S12's null check judges comments against an unrelated act's amendments: 0% *implements* (AI Act) and 1% (DSA), about one third *partial* |

### Adding a new law

The law must be an ordinary legislative procedure (`...(COD)`) with Have Your Say feedback, committee amendments in the ParlTrack dumps
(2019-2024 Parliament) and an adopted final act. Using the Digital Markets Act (`dma`) as the example:

1. **Collect the facts.** The Have Your Say initiative and its publication IDs (2020 consultation, feedback on the proposal), from
   `https://ec.europa.eu/info/law/better-regulation/brpapi/searchInitiatives?text=...` and `.../groupInitiatives/<id>`; the CELEX numbers of the
   proposal and the final act; from the ParlTrack dumps the report ID, how many report amendments are the lead committee's (the rest are plenary
   group amendments), the lead committee, the rapporteurs and the dates.
2. **Write the config.** Copy `data_sources/procedures/dsa.yaml` to `dma.yaml` and fill it in. Pick a `null_procedure` on a different topic
   (it sets the matching threshold). The file is protected: the owner approves it.
3. **Get the comments.**
   ```bash
   .venv/bin/python WebScraping/download_all_feedback.py --procedure dma
   .venv/bin/python WebScraping/parse_and_clean_feedback.py --procedure dma
   ```
4. **Get the legal texts.** Download the proposal and the final act into `data_sources/eurlex/raw/<CELEX>.html`:
   ```bash
   curl -L -H 'Accept: application/xhtml+xml' -H 'Accept-Language: eng' \
        -o data_sources/eurlex/raw/<CELEX>.html https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/<CELEX>
   ```
5. **Give it a database.** Copy `neo4j/docker-compose.dsa.yml` to `docker-compose.dma.yml` and change the project `name:`, the
   `container_name:`, both host ports and the `data-dsa`/`logs-dsa` folders (otherwise it replaces the DSA database), create `.env.dma` (gitignored) with
   `NEO4J_URI`, `NEO4J_USER` and `NEO4J_PASSWORD`, and start it with `docker compose -f neo4j/docker-compose.dma.yml --env-file .env.dma up -d`.
6. **Run it.** `.venv/bin/python -m pipeline.run --procedure dma` (minutes, plus LLM judging: about $0.10 for the DSA). Results go to `data/dma/`.
7. **Check it.** S00 should report no warnings; S02 prints how well the parsed articles match the ParlTrack amendment locations (aim for 90%
   or more exact); open `data/dma/results/demo.html` and `data/dma/results/dashboard.html`.
8. **Optional: 2020 positions.** Track A stance coding needs a codebook `data_sources/procedures/dma_issues.yaml` (format as
   `ai_act_issues.yaml`); without it that analysis is skipped.

Plan for one to two hours, mostly step 1 and any parser fixes the new EUR-Lex HTML needs.

### Limitations

- **Which laws fit.** Only ordinary legislative procedures with Have Your Say feedback, committee amendments and an adopted act; the ParlTrack
  dumps cover the 2019-2024 Parliament. Integrity Watch meetings cover 2020 and June-October 2024 only, so for most laws the access analysis is thin
  (the DSA's committee phase, 2021, is not covered at all).
- **Manual setup.** The facts in step 1 are looked up by hand; nothing yet fills the config from the procedure number. The EUR-Lex download
  and the Neo4j config are manual too.
- **Parser tested on two laws.** The EUR-Lex parser and the amendment-location matching were tuned on the AI Act and checked on the DSA; another
  law's HTML or numbering may need small fixes.
- **One database per law.** Each law needs its own Neo4j instance. Several laws in one graph needs a change to the graph contract.
- **Demo shows one law per build.** Each demo lists its own law as processed and the others as "not processed yet"; the list of known acts is
  hard-coded in `pipeline/s11b_demo.py`.
- **Track A needs a codebook per law.** The DSA has none in the repo yet (a draft is waiting for review), so its 2020-positions analysis is skipped.
- **Reproducibility.** S10's random baselines use a fixed seed, but the pool of MEPs they draw from is read from Neo4j without a fixed order,
  so the baselines can shift slightly between runs. S12's cross-model check makes two LLM calls on every run whose answers do not get cached.
- **Module boundaries.** `tach check` still reports the cross-stage imports listed in `TASKS.md` (boundary debt), so the check fails until
  they are moved into shared modules.

### Next steps

- More laws: the Digital Markets Act and the European Health Data Space, and a resolver that fills `<id>.yaml` from the procedure number.
- Quality: approve a DSA codebook, fix the order of S10's MEP pool, draw S12's null sample at random, clear the boundary debt.
- Online version: several laws in one Neo4j, a small API over it, demo pages that load each law's data on request, and a queue for new laws.

## Configuration

`pipeline/config.py` holds paths, thresholds and defaults and loads the selected law from `data_sources/procedures/<id>.yaml`; models and
secrets come from `.env`, and another law's Neo4j from `.env.<id>` (both gitignored). Notable settings: `LLM_CONCURRENCY`,
`LLM_MAX_PAIRS_PER_TRACK` (2,000), `NULL_PERCENTILE` (99), `VERBATIM_RUN` (12 words). Swapping a judge model is a one-line change in `.env`.

## Caveats

- **Similarity is not causation.** The null baseline, the shared-quote masking and the LLM judge reduce false matches; they do not prove influence.
- **Register figures are 2026, not 2021.** Unmatched organisations have no spend, never zero.
- **Meeting data has a gap.** Integrity Watch covers 2020 and June-October 2024 only, so a missing meeting means "not recorded".
- **Automatic matching.** Organisations are matched to the register without hand-checking; uncertain matches are left unmatched, and a sensitivity check repeats the money analysis on register-number matches only. 206 of 277 organisations are matched.
- **The judge sees a capped sample.** About 2,000 of the strongest Track B echoes are LLM-judged; the rest stay at tier T3. S12: the judge and a
  second model agree on 58% of Track B pairs (kappa 0.35) and 67% of Track A pairs (kappa 0.39); disagreements downgrade the tier.
- **The judge's *partial* is weak evidence.** On 200 unrelated-act pairs above the threshold it says *implements* 0% of the time but *partial*
  36.5% (DSA run: 1% *implements*, 30% *partial*). Echoes therefore need *implements*, or *partial* plus a verbatim run. The null sample is the first 200 pairs
  above the threshold in file order, not a random draw.
- **Random baselines can shift.** S10's baselines (random MEPs, random choices) use a fixed seed, but the MEP pool is read from Neo4j without a
  fixed order, so they can change slightly from run to run.
- **Second act.** The Digital Services Act runs end to end (see [Other laws](#other-laws)); its findings are not summarised here yet.
